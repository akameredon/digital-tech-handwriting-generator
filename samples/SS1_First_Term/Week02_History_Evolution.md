**Class:** SS1  
**Term:** First Term  
**Week:** 2  
**Topic:** History & Evolution of Digital Technologies

---

**Behavioral Learning Objectives**  
By the end of this lesson, students should be able to:  
1. Trace the major stages in the evolution of computers.  
2. State the characteristics of each generation of computers.  
3. Explain how digital technologies have changed from mechanical devices to modern AI systems.  
4. Give examples of technology used in each generation.

---

**Key Concepts / Definitions**  
- **Generation of computers**: Classification of computers according to the technology used in different periods.  
- **First Generation (1940s–1950s)**: Vacuum tubes.  
- **Second Generation (1950s–1960s)**: Transistors.  
- **Third Generation (1960s–1970s)**: Integrated Circuits (ICs).  
- **Fourth Generation (1970s–present)**: Microprocessors.  
- **Fifth Generation (present and future)**: Artificial Intelligence and parallel processing.

---

**Step-by-Step Conceptual Explanation**  

1. Early calculating devices such as the abacus and later mechanical calculators were the ancestors of modern computers.  
2. First-generation computers were very large, used vacuum tubes, consumed a lot of power and produced a lot of heat.  
3. The invention of the transistor made second-generation computers smaller, faster and more reliable.  
4. Integrated circuits allowed third-generation computers to become even smaller and more powerful.  
5. The microprocessor brought computers to homes, schools and offices (fourth generation).  
6. Today we are moving into the fifth generation, where machines can learn and make decisions (AI).

---

**Practical / Worked Example**  
Compare a smartphone in a student’s hand today with the room-sized computers of the 1940s. The same computing power (or more) now fits in a pocket.

---

**Class Activity / Guided Practice**  
In groups, students create a simple timeline showing the five generations of computers and one key invention in each generation.

---

**End-of-Lesson Quick Test**  
1. Which technology was used in first-generation computers?  
2. Name the technology that defined the fourth generation.  
3. What is the main focus of fifth-generation computers?  
4. Why were first-generation computers difficult to use?  
5. True or False: Modern smartphones belong to the first generation of computers.
