**Class:** SS1  
**Term:** Second Term  
**Week:** 1  
**Topic:** Computer Networks

---

**Behavioral Learning Objectives**  
By the end of this lesson, students should be able to:  
1. Define a computer network.  
2. Distinguish between LAN, MAN and WAN.  
3. List the main components of a computer network.  
4. State advantages of computer networks.

---

**Key Concepts / Definitions**  
- **Computer Network**: A collection of computers and other devices connected together to share resources and information.  
- **LAN (Local Area Network)**: A network that covers a small area such as a school, office or home.  
- **MAN (Metropolitan Area Network)**: A network that covers a city or large campus.  
- **WAN (Wide Area Network)**: A network that covers a large geographical area (e.g. the Internet).  
- **Network Interface Card (NIC)**: Hardware that allows a computer to connect to a network.  
- **Router / Switch**: Devices that help direct data between computers on a network.

---

**Step-by-Step Conceptual Explanation**  

1. When computers are connected, they can share files, printers and internet access.  
2. Most school computer laboratories use a LAN.  
3. The Internet is the largest WAN in the world.  
4. Networks make collaboration and resource sharing easier and cheaper.  
5. Understanding networks helps students appreciate how the internet and school systems work.

---

**Practical / Worked Example**  
In a typical school lab:  
- Computers are connected by cables or Wi-Fi (LAN).  
- A shared printer serves many computers.  
- Students can access the internet through a single connection.

---

**Class Activity / Guided Practice**  
Students identify whether the following are LAN, MAN or WAN: school computer lab, a city’s CCTV system, the Internet, home Wi-Fi.

---

**End-of-Lesson Quick Test**  
1. What is a computer network?  
2. What does LAN stand for?  
3. Give one example of a WAN.  
4. Name two devices used in computer networks.  
5. True or False: A home Wi-Fi network is an example of a WAN.
