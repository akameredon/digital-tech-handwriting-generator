**Class:** SS1  
**Term:** Second Term  
**Week:** 9  
**Topic:** Data Privacy & Digital Ethics

---

**Behavioral Learning Objectives**  
By the end of this lesson, students should be able to:  
1. Define data privacy and digital ethics.  
2. Explain why personal data should be protected.  
3. Identify examples of unethical digital behaviour.  
4. State responsible ways of using digital technology.

---

**Key Concepts / Definitions**  
- **Data Privacy**: The right of individuals to control how their personal information is collected, used and shared.  
- **Digital Ethics**: Moral principles that guide the responsible use of digital technology.  
- **Personal data**: Information that can identify a person (name, phone number, address, photos, school records, etc.).  
- **Cyberbullying**: Using digital platforms to harass, threaten or embarrass someone.  
- **Consent**: Giving permission before personal data is collected or used.

---

**Step-by-Step Conceptual Explanation**  

1. Every time we use the internet, phones or apps, we leave digital traces of personal information.  
2. This information can be misused if it falls into the wrong hands.  
3. Digital ethics requires us to respect other people’s privacy, avoid cyberbullying, and use technology honestly.  
4. Students should think carefully before posting photos, comments or personal details online.  
5. Responsible digital citizenship protects both the individual and the community.

---

**Practical / Worked Example**  
Examples of good digital ethics:  
- Asking permission before posting someone’s photo  
- Not sharing other people’s private messages  
- Reporting cyberbullying instead of joining it  
- Using strong privacy settings on social media

---

**Class Activity / Guided Practice**  
Students discuss in groups five situations and decide whether each is ethical or unethical, then share their reasons.

---

**End-of-Lesson Quick Test**  
1. What is data privacy?  
2. What is digital ethics?  
3. Give two examples of personal data.  
4. What is cyberbullying?  
5. True or False: It is acceptable to share a friend’s private messages without permission.

---

**Assignment**  
Write ten rules for responsible and ethical use of digital technology that every secondary school student should follow.
