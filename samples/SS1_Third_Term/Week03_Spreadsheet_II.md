**Class:** SS1  
**Term:** Third Term  
**Week:** 3  
**Topic:** Spreadsheet Applications II – Functions, Charts and Data Analysis

---

**Behavioral Learning Objectives**  
By the end of this lesson, students should be able to:  
1. Use common spreadsheet functions (SUM, AVERAGE, MAX, MIN, COUNT).  
2. Create a simple chart from data.  
3. Interpret basic charts (bar and pie).  
4. Explain how spreadsheets help in decision making.

---

**Key Concepts / Definitions**  
- **Function**: A predefined formula in a spreadsheet (e.g. `=SUM(A1:A5)`).  
- **Chart**: A visual representation of data (bar chart, pie chart, line chart).  
- **Data Analysis**: Examining data to find patterns, totals and comparisons.  
- **Range**: A group of cells (e.g. B2:B10).

---

**Step-by-Step Conceptual Explanation**  

1. Functions save time and reduce calculation errors.  
2. `SUM` adds numbers, `AVERAGE` finds the mean, `MAX` and `MIN` find the highest and lowest values.  
3. Charts make it easier to compare data at a glance.  
4. School teachers and offices use spreadsheets to analyse results, budgets and attendance.  
5. Students who master these tools have an advantage in both academic and future work.

---

**Practical / Worked Example**  
Enter scores for five subjects and use:  
- `=SUM(B2:B6)` for total  
- `=AVERAGE(B2:B6)` for average  
- Insert a bar chart showing the scores

---

**Class Activity / Guided Practice**  
Students create a small table of their weekly expenses, calculate the total and average, and insert a simple pie chart.

---

**End-of-Lesson Quick Test**  
1. What does the SUM function do?  
2. What does AVERAGE do?  
3. Name two types of charts.  
4. Why are charts useful?  
5. True or False: You must calculate totals manually in a spreadsheet.

---

**Assignment**  
Create a spreadsheet showing the marks of 8 students in three subjects. Use functions to find the total and average for each student and insert one chart.
