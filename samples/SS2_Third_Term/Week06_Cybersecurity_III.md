**Class:** SS2  
**Term:** Third Term  
**Week:** 6  
**Topic:** Cybersecurity III – Ethical Hacking & Penetration Testing Basics

---

**Behavioral Learning Objectives**  
By the end of this lesson, students should be able to:  
1. Define ethical hacking.  
2. Explain the difference between ethical and criminal hacking.  
3. State the purpose of penetration testing.  
4. List basic rules of responsible security testing.

---

**Key Concepts / Definitions**  
- **Ethical Hacking**: Legally authorised testing of systems to find security weaknesses so they can be fixed.  
- **Penetration Testing (Pentest)**: A simulated attack on a system to evaluate its security.  
- **Vulnerability**: A weakness that can be exploited.  
- **Authorisation**: Permission given by the owner of a system before testing begins.

---

**Step-by-Step Conceptual Explanation**  

1. Not all hacking is criminal. Ethical hackers help organisations protect themselves.  
2. They only work with written permission and report the problems they find.  
3. Penetration testing is a controlled way to discover weaknesses before real attackers do.  
4. Students must understand that testing systems without permission is illegal under Nigerian law.  
5. Learning these concepts prepares interested students for future careers in cybersecurity.

---

**Practical / Worked Example**  
A school asks a security professional to test its student portal. The professional is given written permission, finds a weak password policy, reports it, and the school fixes it. This is ethical hacking.

---

**Class Activity / Guided Practice**  
Students discuss the difference between ethical and criminal actions in groups and list three rules an ethical hacker must follow.

---

**End-of-Lesson Quick Test**

2. What is penetration testing?  
3. Why is authorisation important?  
4. Give one difference between ethical and criminal hacking.  
5. True or False: It is legal to test any website without permission as long as you report the findings.
